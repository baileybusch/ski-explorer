# Powder-day call: decision checklist

Source: method distilled from mammothsnowman.com — Ted Schlaepfer, CCM (Powder Forecast, run since 2008/09 to help skiers plan powder days) and Steve Taylor ("The Mammoth Snowman"). Paraphrased; numbers are theirs unless tagged **[general knowledge]**. Uses `reading-snow-quality`, `reading-forecasts`, `storm-setups-and-patterns`.

> **Regional adjustment:** Depth tiers, wind-hold thresholds and storm-day patterns below are calibrated on Mammoth (Sierra). Load `regional-snow-climates` and adjust: e.g. Japan's frequent light refills, Colorado's lighter but less frequent snow on a shallow base, and maritime regions where freezing level vs. base elevation decides the call. Steenburgh & Alcott's ≥25 cm (~10 in) "deep-powder day" is a cross-region reference point.

## When to use
- Choosing the best day in a window, or grading one specific morning.
- Deciding whether to recommend storm skiing vs. the day after.

## Core principles
1. **A powder day is judged on the morning report.** Snow counted is what fell in the prior 24 h at the 6–7 AM report. A storm ending overnight = powder next morning.
2. **Grade it, don't just say yes/no.** The source uses tiers: "low-end powder conditions" (a few inches, often on limited terrain), "enough for powder conditions" (~6–9"), a "mountain-wide powder day" (over a foot).
3. **Quality and depth both count.** "An inch or more of liquid... enough for powder conditions under high liquid-to-snow ratios." Cold, light snow stretches a little water a long way; cement needs more depth to feel like powder (but builds base).
4. **Wind can steal the day.** Lifts usually go on wind hold at **sustained 45–55 mph or gusts over 65 mph**. Storm days often mean limited terrain; "the Top might not open until" the next day.
5. **Storm day vs. day after.** Storm days ("Riders of the Storm" days) can be great on open terrain but with poor visibility and limited lifts. The day after — "clearing skies... light winds," cold — is often the best: full terrain, snow stays good.
6. **Steepness vs. depth.** When powder gets deep, low-angle terrain bogs down; mid and upper mountain is where to find it.
7. **Wind buff counts for something.** Even with no new snow, breezy westerly winds can deliver "wind buff up top" — a minor freshening, not a powder day.

## Checklist (score each, then grade)
1. **New snow by report time** at the elevation the user will ski:
   - 0–1": no powder (maybe wind buff).
   - ~2–5": **low-end powder** (limited terrain/low tide early season).
   - ~6–11": **powder day** on most open terrain.
   - 12"+: **mountain-wide powder day** (if lifts open).
   *(Tier cut-offs are a synthesis of how the source labels days, not his exact thresholds.)*
2. **Quality:** ratio ≥12:1 → light, upgrade; ~8:1 → heavy, downgrade for feel (upgrade if the base needs coverage).
3. **Wind:** gusts >65 mph or sustained 45–55 on storm day → expect lift holds; upper lifts may open a day late. Strong wind during snowfall → wind-packed up top, shallower than water implies.
4. **Timing:** snow ending overnight/early = fresh at first chair; snow starting mid-morning = better the next day.
5. **Aftermath:** clearing + cold + light wind = stays good; warm sun/spring = goes heavy/sticky by afternoon **[general knowledge: sun-facing aspects go first]**.
6. **Base/coverage:** early season "low tide" → limited terrain, rocks; a big storm may be "great base snow" rather than a blower day.
7. **Confidence:** from `reading-forecasts` (1–5). Below 3 → present as "possible," name the day it'll firm up.
8. **Ops & access [general knowledge + source notes]:** storm protocols can delay lower lifts (Mammoth: lower lifts after 9:30 AM on storm days); highway chain controls/closures in big storms. Mention when travel is risky — "only cancel if travel is dangerous."

## Output
- **Grade:** No / Low-end / Powder day / Mountain-wide powder day (+ confidence 1–5).
- **Best day** in the window and why. **Best terrain** (elevation, aspect, steepness).
- **Watch-outs:** wind holds, visibility, cement, roads.

## Worked examples
**A. Day after a big dump (Ted, Nov 17 2026):** report totals Thu 24–28" and Fri 15–20" (snow Wed through Thu evening, "blizzard conditions" Wed night), ending overnight Thu; dry Fri and Sat, seasonably cold. Call: Thursday = storm skiing, likely wind holds and limited terrain; **Friday = best day** (snow ended overnight, ~3–4 ft total at Main) — mountain-wide powder as terrain opens; Saturday still excellent. Confidence 4.
**B. Small storm (Ted, Nov 13 2026):** 4–5" Monday, dry Tuesday → "at least low-end powder conditions on limited terrain/low tide" Tuesday. Confidence 3.
**C. Spring storm (Ted, Apr 14 2026):** ECM EPS ~0.65" → 6–9", "enough for powder conditions... best chance on Tuesday," but "the Top might not open until Wednesday." Call: powder day Tue on lower/mid; Wed for upper mountain.
**D. Warm wet storm (Snowman, Nov 2026):** 9–13" at 8:1→11:1, 45–60 mph gusts, chair 1 likely on wind hold. Call: base-builder, not a blower; Saturday (clearing, light winds) is "a great day to get out."

## Pitfalls
- Calling a powder day from summit totals when the lower lifts are all that will run.
- Ignoring wind holds — 18" with every upper lift closed is not an 18" day for most skiers.
- Recommending the storm day when visibility and lift closures make the next day better.
- Treating a 10-day-out "maybe" as a plan; say when to check back.
