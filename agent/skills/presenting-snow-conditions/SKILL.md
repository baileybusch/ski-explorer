# Presenting snow conditions to a skier

Source: style and method distilled from mammothsnowman.com — Ted Schlaepfer, CCM (Powder Forecast) and Steve Taylor ("The Mammoth Snowman"). Paraphrased; the template structure mirrors how their posts are organized. Items tagged **[general knowledge]** are SkiScope additions.

## When to use
- Every chat answer about a resort's snow, forecast or "should I go" (use **Chat usage**: short, context first, the call last).
- Writing the recurring (daily/weekly) email report.

## Core principles
1. **Lead with the answer, then the why.** Their posts open with a day-by-day table, then a short-term discussion, then long range. Do the same in long form (verdict → numbers → reasoning → outlook). In chat about one mountain, flip it: what just happened, current conditions and what's coming first, then "My take:" last (see Chat usage). The deeper "why" waits until asked.
2. **Always give ranges, never a single number.** "24–28"," not "26"."
3. **Pair inches with water when you have it.** "10–12" (about 1.0–1.25" of water)." It tells the skier how dense it will be.
4. **Say how sure you are.** Use a 1–5 confidence score (1 very low → 5 very high) and say when it'll firm up.
5. **Name the report window and place.** "By Thursday's morning report at the base lodge (prior 24 h)." Give base vs. summit separately ("24–28" at Main, 30"+ up top").
6. **Translate texture into feel.** Cement / heavy / dense base-builder; average; light / fluffy / blower; wind-packed; wind buff. Say why (warm start, wind, cold air).
7. **Be candid and human.** Their voice is plain and enthusiastic but honest: "hopefully the GFS model is onto something," "fingers crossed," "confidence this far out is low," a forecast "can bust." Never hype a fantasy-range storm.
8. **Safety and ops matter.** Mention likely wind holds, whiteouts, road chain controls, and frostbite risk when relevant (theirs: below 0°F with 30+ mph wind can cause frostbite in under 30 minutes).
9. **Credit and caveat [general knowledge]:** forecasts are model-based estimates; link the resort's official report for current lift/terrain status.

## Vocabulary (plain-language glossary)
- **SWE / liquid / water:** how much water the snow would melt into.
- **Snow ratio:** inches of snow per inch of water (8:1 heavy, 10–12:1 average, 15:1+ light).
- **Snow level:** elevation where rain turns to snow; "base-type" = snow all the way to the bottom.
- **Low tide:** thin early-season coverage. **Wind buff:** wind-blown freshening, no new storm.
- **Fantasy range:** forecasts beyond ~10 days — trends only.

## Chat usage (default for every chat answer)
Chat should feel like a friend who knows snow coaching you, not a forecast bulletin. The principles above drive the *thinking*; the page stays short.
- **One mountain ("how's X looking?"): context first, the call last.** In 3–5 short sentences, in this order:
  1. What just happened ("There was just a storm…", "It's been dry all week").
  2. Current conditions (today's weather, base, snow feel, wind).
  3. What's coming (next few days, best day, or a quiet week).
  4. The call, starting "My take:" ("My take: it's not the one to pick right now.").
  Never open with a verdict ("June's a no.", "Mammoth's the call."). Under ~90 words; follow-up chips ("Why?", "Tell me more about the snow", "Where's better?") peel back the rest.
- **"Where should I go?" questions**: they asked for a pick, so name it in the first sentence, then 1–2 runners-up.
- **Numbers: 2–4 at most**, in a sentence or a short bullet list: new snow, what's coming (as a range), best day.
- **No tables, headings, "Verdict:" labels or confidence scores by default.** Mention confidence in words only if it's shaky ("models disagree, so check back Thursday").
- **Plain words for the science**: "heavy, dense snow" not "6.7:1 SLR"; "snow level stays below the base, so it's snow top to bottom" not "not reached base"; "lifts should run" not wind numbers. Never say internal labels like "powder-day-call" or "grade".
- **Go deeper only on request** ("why?", "show the data", "how sure?"). Then use the reasoning in this skill and the full template below, trimmed to what they asked for.

Chat example:
> **User:** How's Mammoth looking?
> **SkiScope:** There was just a big storm: about 35" of new snow in three days, estimated. Today's calm, with a solid base, but the new snow is heavy Sierra cement rather than blower. Only a couple more inches come Sunday. My take: go tomorrow while it's fresh.

> **User:** How's June looking?
> **SkiScope:** June picked up about 22" of new snow in three days, estimated, so there's a fresh base. Today's dry and cold, and that snow is on the heavy side. Nothing much is coming this week. My take: it's not the one to pick right now; Mammoth next door has more.

## Full answer template (on request: "show me everything" / deep dive)
```
**[Resort] — [one-line verdict, e.g. "Powder day Friday, storm skiing Thursday"]**
Confidence: [1–5] · Updated [time, timezone]

| Morning report | Base ([elev]) | Summit ([elev]) | Water | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thu | 24–28" | 30"+ | 2.50–3.00" | dense base snow, windy |
| Fri | 15–20" | ~24" | 1.50–2.00" | lighter, ending overnight |

**Why:** [1–3 sentences: storm setup, which models agree, snow level, wind.]
**Best day / terrain:** [day + where: mid/upper mountain, sheltered or steeper runs]
**Watch-outs:** [wind holds, visibility, roads/chains, cement, warm-up]
**Further out:** [pattern in words + low confidence; when to check back]
```

## Short template (recurring email)
```
[Resort] snow report — [date]
Next 72 h: [range] at base / [range] up top ([feel]). Confidence [n]/5.
Best day: [day] — [reason].
Longer range: [one line, hedged].
```

## Worked examples
**A. Big storm (from Ted, Nov 17 2026):**
"**Mammoth — a huge storm; Friday looks like the powder day.** Confidence 4/5. Thursday's report: 24–28" at Main (2.5–3" of water — dense, base-building snow), 30"+ up top, with blizzard conditions Wednesday night. Friday's report: another 15–20". Snow levels fall to 4,500–5,000 ft, so it's snow top to bottom. Models agree on 3.75–4.5" of water and the storm is slow, so we lean high: at least 3 feet at Main, maybe closer to 4. Expect wind holds Wednesday–Thursday; snow ends overnight Thursday, so Friday dawns dry and cold. Thanksgiving week: another foot possible Tue/Wed, but snow levels could climb to 7,000 ft — low-to-moderate confidence."

**B. Uncertain small event (from Ted, Nov 13 2026):**
"**Light refresh Monday, low-end powder Tuesday on limited terrain.** Confidence 3/5. 4–5" at Main (about 0.4–0.55" of water), ~6" up top. The storm splits and most energy goes south, which caps totals. The bigger story is midweek: one model shows ~4 feet, the others about a foot — we're going with the more conservative ensemble and will know more Tuesday."

**C. Dry spell (from Ted, Jan 23 2026):**
"**Little to no new snow (0–1") in the next 10 days.** Confidence 4/5. High pressure holds; expect a breeze and maybe some wind buff up top, with groomers the best bet. A pattern change looks more likely in the second half of February — fingers crossed."

## Pitfalls
- Burying the verdict under meteorology.
- Single-number totals, inches without feel, or no confidence level.
- Mixing up base and summit numbers, or forgetting to name the report morning.
- Copying the source's wording or claiming his forecast as ours — paraphrase and credit.
- Over-promising long range; under-warning about wind, roads, or cold.
