# European Alps — north side (Switzerland, Austria, France, Bavaria)

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Alpine**, mostly Atlantic-fed. Precipitation depends on flow direction against the Alpine arc and on elevation [src: MeteoSwiss; Wikipedia, Climate of the Alps].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Westerly Atlantic flow is milder and wetter; northerly polar flow is colder and drier, with smaller totals [src: MeteoSwiss Nordstaulage]. So ratios run roughly 8:1–12:1 in mild westerlies and 15:1+ in cold northerly flow [GK, approx.].
- Inner-Alpine valleys (Valais, Engadin, Tyrol's interior) are drier, colder [GK].

## Storm setups and flow ("Stau" types)
- **Nordstau / Nordweststau / Weststau**: moist flow nearly perpendicular to the Alps is forced up the north slopes, giving heavy precipitation there; south of the Alps air sinks and dries (north foehn) [src: MeteoSwiss].
  - **Northwest** flow often gives the **highest totals**, covering the whole north slope from the Vaud Alps to Toggenburg [src].
  - **West** flow is Atlantic-moist; **north** flow is colder and drier, mainly hitting central/eastern north slopes [src].
  - The most productive events involve an active warm front, or a cold front followed by an active trough [src].
- Arlberg (St. Anton, Lech) and central Switzerland (Engelberg) are classic NW-stau catchers [GK].
- Southern stau can spill over the crest into some northern-side resorts ("übergreifender Südstau") [src: MeteoSwiss research report].

## Snow-killers
- **South foehn**: during southern stau the north side gets warm, dry, gusty downslope wind [src: MeteoSwiss foehn page]. Melts and wind-affects snow [GK].
- Mild Atlantic warm sectors with high snow lines, rain to 2,000 m+ in some storms [GK].
- Long high-pressure spells (inversions, sun crusts) [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Resort bases often 1,000–1,800 m, summits 2,500–3,800 m (glaciers higher) [GK, approx.]. Low-elevation resorts (<1,500 m) are increasingly rain-prone; the snow line is the key forecast variable [GK].

## Seasonality
- Main snow Dec–Mar; high/glacier areas into April–May [GK].

## Powder-day profile
- Off-piste culture: most powder skiing is outside marked runs, so avalanche bulletins matter for every powder call [GK].
- Best: cold NW stau ending with clearing and light wind [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- ENSO influence on the Alps is weak [GK]. **NAO** shapes the storm track over Europe; positive NAO steers Atlantic storms north, negative NAO tends to shift them south (benefiting the southern Alps more) [src: NOAA CPC NAO page for the pattern; Alps effect GK].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Mixed; inner-Alpine areas behave more continental (persistent weak layers), outer north slopes more maritime [GK]. Danger levels 1–5 on the European scale [GK; see EAWS].

## Example resorts
- **St. Anton/Lech (AT)**, **Engelberg (CH)**, **Chamonix (FR)** [GK].
