# Interior BC / Canadian Rockies

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- Western Canada runs from **maritime** (South Coast Mountains) to **transitional** (Columbia Mountains: Revelstoke, Rogers Pass, Kicking Horse) to **continental** (Rocky Mountains: Banff, Lake Louise) [src: Haegeli thesis, hosted on avalanche.org].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Columbias: lighter than the coast, often roughly 10:1 to 15:1 with frequent moderate storms [GK, approx.].
- Rockies: cold, drier snow, often 15:1+, but much less water [GK, approx.].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Westerly/southwesterly Pacific flow**: moisture that survives the Coast Mountains is wrung out over the Monashees/Selkirks (Revelstoke, Rogers Pass) [GK].
- The Rockies' Continental Divide gets upslope from W/SW flow on the BC side (Kicking Horse, Fernie, Sunshine Village area); the Alberta side is drier and rain-shadowed [GK].
- **Arctic fronts / upslope from the east**: when Arctic air pushes in from Alberta, easterly upslope can bring snow to the Front Ranges [GK].
- Warm ARs reach the interior too; the November 2021 AR caused severe flooding from rain-on-snow in the Fraser Basin [src: Wikipedia, Atmospheric river].

## Snow-killers
- **Chinooks**: warm, dry foehn winds down the east slopes; a strong one can make ~30 cm (1 ft) of snow nearly vanish in a day [src: Wikipedia, Chinook wind].
- Warm ARs raising freezing levels, rain on lower slopes [src/GK].
- Extreme cold snaps (−30 °C range) close lifts and facet the snowpack [GK].
- Wind in Rockies alpine terrain [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Bases are roughly 500–1,700 m; summits to ~2,700 m [GK, approx.]. Revelstoke's base sits low, so its lower runs are rain-prone in warm storms while the alpine stays snowy [GK].

## Seasonality
- Snow accumulates Nov–Apr; deepest packs Feb–Mar [GK]. Banff Sunshine reports up to ~30 ft (9 m+) of annual snowfall [src: Wikipedia].

## Powder-day profile
- Columbias: frequent refreshes, deep tree skiing; powder days are common in Dec–Feb [GK].
- Rockies: shallower base; light snow skis great when it falls, but there's less of it and the cold preserves it [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- La Niña winters tend to be colder and snowier in western Canada; El Niño tends warmer with less snow [GK, commonly stated by Environment Canada; verify per season].
- Haegeli's analysis ties ENSO phase to snow-climate deviations across western Canada [src: Haegeli thesis].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Columbia Mountains: transitional; persistent surface-hoar layers are a signature problem [src: Haegeli thesis lists faceted grains, surface hoar and crusts as the main persistent weaknesses in the region].
- Rockies: continental; shallow, faceted, weak basal layers; persistent slab problems can last all season [src: Avalanche.org continental page].

## Example resorts
- **Revelstoke**: transitional Columbias, huge vertical [GK].
- **Kicking Horse**: Purcells, drier than Revelstoke [GK].
- **Banff Sunshine / Lake Louise**: continental Rockies, cold and lighter snow [GK; Sunshine snowfall per Wikipedia].
