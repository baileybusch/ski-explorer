# Utah — Wasatch Range

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Intermountain/transitional**; the Wasatch is Avalanche.org's example [src]. Plus **Great Salt Lake effect**.

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Alta's new-snow water content: mean **8.4%**, median **7.6%** (Nov–Apr); Dec–Mar mean 8.2% [src: Steenburgh & Alcott 2008]. That's ~12:1 on average.
- On deep-powder days (≥25 cm) mean water content is **7.7%**; 40.8% of those days are low-water (<6.7%) and only 12.6% high-water (>11.1%) [src].
- Lake-effect snow is about **5%** of total snowfall but very light, about 5% density, per Steenburgh [src: KUTV interview].
- Colorado's snow is even drier than Utah's on average, but that's not necessarily better for skiing [src: Steenburgh via KUTV].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Northwest flow** behind cold fronts is the classic Cottonwood producer; storms often start warm (denser) and get colder (lighter), the "right-side-up" stack ideal for flotation [src: Steenburgh via KUTV].
- **Great Salt Lake effect**: cold NW flow over the unfrozen salty lake adds bands that "polish off the end of the storm" [src: KUTV].
- Little Cottonwood (Alta, Snowbird) gets the most; Park City sits in the lee — annual precip drops to 54 cm at Park City vs. 136 cm in Alta town [src: Steenburgh & Alcott].
- Southwest flow ahead of troughs brings warmer, denser snow and wind [GK].

## Snow-killers
- Long inversions under high pressure: valley pollution, warm ridgetops, sun crusts [GK].
- Strong SW wind ahead of fronts; occasional warm ARs/rain at lower elevations [GK].
- Dry spells; storms that split or track south [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Alta 8,530–11,068 ft [src: Wikipedia]; most Wasatch bases ~6,500–8,500 ft [GK]. Rain is uncommon at Cottonwood elevations mid-winter but possible in early/late season [GK].

## Seasonality
- Alta averages about **543 in** [src: Wikipedia/Alta]; Steenburgh & Alcott give a mean Nov–Apr snowfall of 1,312 cm at the ski area [src].
- Deep-powder days (≥25 cm): **17.4 per season** at Alta on average, about one every 10 days [src]. Snowbird set a record 776 in in 2010–11 [src: Wikipedia].
- Peak months roughly Dec–Mar [GK].

## Powder-day profile
- Frequent deep, low-to-average-density storms; a foot-plus overnight is a normal powder day here [src: Steenburgh stats].
- Interlodge/road closures (SR-210 in Little Cottonwood) on big days; check UDOT [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- ENSO signal is weak in northern Utah; Utah sits between the El Niño-favored south and La Niña-favored north [src: NOAA climate.gov map; GK].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Intermountain: persistent weak layers less common and shorter-lived than in Colorado, but sometimes last all season (e.g., early-season facets) [src: Avalanche.org intermountain page].

## Example resorts
- **Alta**, **Snowbird** (Little Cottonwood, deepest/most frequent) [src].
- **Park City / Deer Valley** (lee side, less snow) [src: precip contrast in Steenburgh & Alcott].
