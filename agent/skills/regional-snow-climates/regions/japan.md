# Japan — Hokkaido and Honshu

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Sea-effect snow under the East Asian winter monsoon**: cold Siberian air crosses the warmer Sea of Japan and is lifted by mountains [src: JMA Hokkaido climate page; Japan Weather Association].
- Mean annual snowfall in the "Gosetsu Chitai" (heavy-snow zone) exceeds 600 cm in some near-sea-level cities and 1,300 cm in some mountain areas; snow depth can reach 7 m in the mountains [src: Steenburgh et al., BAMS, "Sea-effect precipitation: Japan's Gosetsu Chitai"].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- **Hokkaido**: persistent sub-zero temperatures and steady cold inflow keep crystals light and intact — "Japow" [src: JWA]. Ratios often high (roughly 15:1–25:1+) [GK, approx.].
- **Honshu (Hokuriku, Niigata, Nagano)**: closer to 0 °C, so snow is wetter and heavier, piling up fast [src: JWA]. Higher Nagano resorts (Hakuba upper mountain, Shiga Kogen) lighter than coastal low areas [GK].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Winter monsoon pattern (西高東低, high west / low east)**: strong NW winds out of the Siberian High bring heavy snow to the Sea of Japan side and sun to the Pacific side [src: JMA Hokkaido page].
- **JPCZ (Japan Sea Polar-air-mass Convergence Zone)**: a ~1,000 km convergence band; when it forms, snow clouds intensify rapidly and can dump locally heavy snow on Honshu's Sea of Japan side [src: JWA].
- Wind speed shifts bands: weak winds favor coastal snow; stronger winds push snow inland [src: JMA Sapporo].
- **Small Ishikari Bay lows** can dump tens of cm in hours and are hard to predict [src: JMA Sapporo].
- **Pacific-side lows (南岸低気圧)** passing south of Honshu bring heavy, wet snow to the Pacific side and Kanto [GK].

## Snow-killers
- Warm sectors of lows crossing the Sea of Japan bring rain, especially in Honshu and late season [GK].
- Strong monsoon winds close lifts and gondolas (Niseko's upper lifts often wind-hold) [GK].
- Southerly winds around a low bring wetter snow [src: JMA Sapporo, per search synthesis; GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Many resorts are low (bases ~200–1,000 m, summits ~1,000–2,000 m) [GK, approx.]. Cold air masses at about −15 to −20 °C aloft drive the snow [src: Japanese government "Highlighting Japan"].

## Seasonality
- Main powder season roughly mid-December to mid-February; snowfall frequency increases through autumn [src: JMA Hokkaido; GK for dates].

## Powder-day profile
- Frequent daily refills of 10–30 cm rather than rare dumps; trees and gentle-to-moderate pitches ski best [GK].
- Check wind first: in strong monsoon outbreaks the best snow is in sheltered trees lower down [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- La Niña winters tend to be colder in Japan with stronger monsoon outbreaks; El Niño tends warmer [GK; JMA TCC ENSO pages: https://ds.data.jma.go.jp/tcc/tcc/products/elnino/].
- Negative Arctic Oscillation favors cold-air outbreaks [GK].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Deep, frequently loaded snowpack; storm slabs, wind slabs and glide avalanches are common; persistent weak layers less dominant [GK].
