# Colorado / Central Rockies

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Continental**; Colorado Rockies are Avalanche.org's example: cold, clear, infrequent low-density snow, shallow faceted snowpack [src].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Judson & Doesken (2000), six sheltered sites in CO/WY: site-mean new-snow density **72–103 kg/m³** (≈7–10% water, ~10:1–14:1); 72% of daily densities between 50–100 kg/m³; ~5% below 40 kg/m³ [src].
- Steamboat Springs lowest mean (72 kg/m³), with occasional very deep, very light falls (36 cm at 23 kg/m³) [src]. Wolf Creek Pass highest (103 kg/m³) [src].
- Density decreases with colder temperatures (correlation 0.52); orography causes big differences over short distances [src].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Northwest flow** favors the northern and central mountains (Steamboat, Vail, Summit County) [GK; OpenSnow Colorado discusses this regularly].
- **Southwest flow** favors the San Juans (Wolf Creek, Telluride, Silverton) [GK].
- **Upslope (easterly) storms** with Arctic air/low to the south: Front Range and east-of-Divide areas (Eldora, sometimes Loveland/Winter Park) can get big, light totals while the west side gets little [GK].
- Lower water per storm than coastal ranges; frequency of deep days is low — Berthoud Pass averages only **3.8** deep-powder days per season vs. Alta's 17.4 [src: Steenburgh & Alcott].

## Snow-killers
- Wind: high, exposed terrain gets scoured and wind-slabbed [GK].
- Long dry spells under ridges; strong sun and low humidity cause sublimation and sun crusts [GK].
- Chinook/downslope winds east of the Divide [src: Wikipedia, Chinook wind; GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- High bases (~8,000–10,800 ft) and summits to ~12,000–13,000 ft [GK]. Rain is rare in mid-winter; early/late-season rain at lower bases possible [GK].

## Seasonality
- Northern mountains peak roughly Dec–Mar; Front Range upslope most common in March–April [GK].
- Wolf Creek averages about **430 in**, the most in Colorado; record 807 in in 1978–79 [src: Wikipedia].

## Powder-day profile
- Light snow; 6–10 in of cold snow can ski well on groomed/settled base [GK]. Early-season rocky base makes depth more important [GK].
- I-70 traffic and closures matter on storm days [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- ENSO effects are weak and split: southern Colorado leans wetter in El Niño, northern mountains lean wetter in La Niña [GK, approx.; NOAA climate.gov map shows the north–south split for the US].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Continental: shallow, faceted, persistent weak layers; danger remains long after storms; human-triggered slides common [src: Avalanche.org continental page].

## Example resorts
- **Steamboat** (light-snow reputation) [src: Judson & Doesken site data].
- **Vail / Summit County** (NW flow) [GK].
- **Wolf Creek** (SW flow, most snow, densest) [src].
