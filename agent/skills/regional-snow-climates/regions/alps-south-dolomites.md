# Southern Alps / Dolomites / Italy (incl. Ticino, Valle d'Aosta, Piedmont, Lombardy)

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Alpine, Mediterranean-fed**. Fewer storm days than the north side but some of the biggest single events [src: OpenSnow Südstau; MeteoSwiss].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Mediterranean moisture often means **wetter, denser snow and higher snow levels**, especially early season when the sea is warm [src: OpenSnow Südstau]. Cold southern stau events can produce large, good-quality snowfalls [src].
- Dolomites in cold northerly/easterly flows: lighter but shallow [GK].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Südstau (southern stau)**: SW–SE upper flow nearly perpendicular to the Alpine arc, with a low **west of Switzerland** pumping mild, very humid Mediterranean air; heavy precipitation on the southern slopes, foehn and dry weather north [src: MeteoSwiss].
- **Genoa lows**: cyclones form or deepen south of the Alps over the Gulf of Genoa/Ligurian Sea/Po Valley, often triggered as Atlantic air enters via the Rhône valley; many stall or leave a residual trough [src: Wikipedia, Genoa low]. These are the main big-snow producers for the southern Alps and Dolomites [GK].
- Convergence zones in the southern Alps enhance lift; OpenSnow notes two main convergence zones [src: OpenSnow Südstau].
- **Northerly flow** dries out the south side with north foehn [src: MeteoSwiss].

## Snow-killers
- **North foehn** (warm, dry, windy downslope) during Nordstau [src: MeteoSwiss].
- High snow levels in warm Mediterranean storms; rain at lower bases [src: OpenSnow; GK].
- Long dry, sunny periods are common; reliance on snowmaking (Dolomiti Superski) [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Dolomites bases ~1,200–1,700 m, summits ~2,500–3,300 m (Marmolada) [GK, approx.]. Snow line can swing 1,000–2,000 m+ in southern events [GK].

## Seasonality
- Southern stau events common in autumn and early winter and again in spring; midwinter often drier and sunnier [GK; OpenSnow notes stronger Südstau early season].

## Powder-day profile
- Fewer, bigger events; when a Genoa low hits with cold air in place, the Dolomites, Ticino, Valle d'Aosta's southern side and Livigno can get the best of the Alps [GK].
- Groomers are the norm; off-piste powder needs bulletin checks [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- ENSO weak [GK]. Negative NAO tends to favor southern storm tracks into the Mediterranean (good for the southern Alps) [GK, see NOAA CPC NAO].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Often shallower, sunnier snowpack between big events → facets and crusts; big southern dumps load it fast [GK].

## Example resorts
- **Cortina d'Ampezzo**, **Val Gardena/Alta Badia** (Dolomites), **Livigno** (Lombardy) [GK].
