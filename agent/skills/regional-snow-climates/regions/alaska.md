# Alaska — coastal (Alyeska, Valdez/Chugach) vs. interior

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Coastal Chugach / Kenai (Alyeska, Turnagain, Thompson Pass/Valdez):** maritime, extremely snowy [src: Alyeska and Thompson Pass articles; GK]. Thompson Pass is often described as having an intermountain-like snowpack on its drier interior side [GK].
- **Interior (Fairbanks area):** cold, dry, continental; light snowfall, very cold, short days [GK].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Coast: dense, wind-affected snow common; ratios often ~8:1 to 12:1, lighter in cold northerly outflow [GK, approx.].
- Interior: very cold, very light snow but low totals [GK].

## Storm setups and flow
- Driven by the **Aleutian Low**: storms from the Gulf of Alaska slam into the coastal ranges with south/southeast flow [src: Wikipedia, Alyeska Resort, cites the Aleutian Low].
- Coastal ranges squeeze out enormous totals; Thompson Pass averages ~500 in, Alaska's snowiest station, with a 62-inch single-day record [src: Wikipedia, Thompson Pass].
- **Cold outflow** (northerly gap winds from the interior) brings clear, cold, windy weather and wind-scoured snow [GK].

## Snow-killers
- Warm Gulf storms bringing rain to low-elevation bases — Alyeska's base is ~250 ft above sea level [src: Wikipedia].
- Wind: violent gap winds, wind slabs, scoured ridges [GK].
- Short daylight (Dec–Jan) and flat light limit skiing [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Bases near sea level (Alyeska) to a few thousand feet; heli terrain to ~6,000–7,000 ft in the Chugach [GK, approx.]. Rain to mid-mountain in warm storms is common at sea-level bases [GK].

## Seasonality
- Alyeska averages about 209 in at the base and >650 in at the top of the Glacier Bowl Express, record 939 in [src: Wikipedia].
- Heli season in Valdez/Haines mostly **Feb/March–April**, when daylight returns and the pack stabilizes [GK].

## Powder-day profile
- Coast: deep but dense; best after a cold front clears a storm, with light wind [GK].
- Weather windows matter more than totals for heli operations (visibility, wind) [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- El Niño tends to bring warmer Alaska winters, La Niña colder [GK; NOAA climate.gov describes the warm El Niño signal for Alaska — verify per season].
- Pacific Decadal Oscillation and Aleutian Low strength strongly shape coastal snow [GK].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Coastal: deep, maritime; storm slabs and wind slabs dominate, along with wet slides in warm storms [src: Avalanche.org maritime page; GK].
- Interior: shallow, heavily faceted (depth hoar) snowpack [GK].
