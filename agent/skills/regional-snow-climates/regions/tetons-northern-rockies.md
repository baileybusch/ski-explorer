# Tetons / Northern Rockies (Wyoming, Montana, Idaho)

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Intermountain/transitional**; Avalanche.org uses the Teton Range as its photo example [src]. Western Montana/Idaho lean transitional; the Montana Front and Absarokas lean continental [GK].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Teton Pass, WY: mean new-snow density **82 kg/m³** (~8% water, ~12:1) in Judson & Doesken's sites [src].
- Cold storms commonly produce lighter snow than the Sierra/Cascades but more water than Colorado [GK, approx.].

## Storm setups and flow
- **West/southwest flow** with Pacific moisture through the Snake River Plain: the Tetons and Grand Targhee (west side) get direct upslope [GK].
- **Northwest flow** behind cold fronts brings colder, lighter snow [GK].
- Big Sky gets less moisture than Jackson; northwest Montana (Whitefish) sees more Pacific influence and fog [GK].
- Arctic outbreaks from Canada: extreme cold, light snow; upslope on east-facing ranges [GK].

## Snow-killers
- Warm ARs/rain at lower bases in early/late season [GK].
- Chinook/downslope winds east of the Divide in Montana [src: Wikipedia, Chinook wind].
- Wind on ridgelines (Jackson's upper tram, Big Sky's Lone Peak) [GK].
- Deep cold snaps (−20 °F and colder) close lifts and facet snow [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Jackson Hole base ~6,300 ft, summit ~10,450 ft; Big Sky summit ~11,166 ft [GK, approx.]. Valley inversions are common; rain is uncommon mid-winter above ~7,000 ft [GK].

## Seasonality
- Peak snowfall roughly Dec–Feb; deep pack by March [GK].

## Powder-day profile
- Frequent moderate storms; Grand Targhee often gets more and lighter snow than the Jackson Hole side [GK]. Steep terrain needs good stability and visibility [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- La Niña tends wetter/colder for the northern Rockies; El Niño drier [src: NOAA climate.gov ENSO page].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Intermountain: mix of storm-snow and persistent problems; persistent layers can linger some seasons [src: Avalanche.org intermountain page]. Montana's colder ranges trend continental [GK].

## Example resorts
- **Jackson Hole**, **Grand Targhee** (WY) [GK].
- **Big Sky** (MT) [GK]. **Whitefish** (MT) for the wetter northwest.
