# Sierra Nevada / California

Tags: **[src]** = linked source says it; **[MS]** = Mammoth Snowman forecasters (Ted Schlaepfer CCM / Steve Taylor), mammothsnowman.com; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Maritime** snow climate [src: Avalanche.org maritime page shows the Sierra as an example]. Warm-ish storms, big water totals, deep snowpack, lots of sun between storms [GK].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Ratios run about **6:1 in warm storms to 20:1 in cold ones** [MS FAQ]. Working values: ~8:1 warm start, 10–12:1 typical, 11–14:1 colder, 12–16:1 cold northern troughs [MS].
- "Sierra cement" = heavy, high-water snow, common in atmospheric-river storms; great for base [MS].
- Sierra and Cascade snow is wetter than Utah's (Alta mean 8.4% water) [src: Steenburgh, quoted by KUTV].

## Storm setups and flow
- Best: **southwest flow with a strong jet**; AR storms from that angle hit the crest hardest and spill over to the east side [MS].
- Atmospheric rivers supply ~30–40% of US West Coast precipitation and snowpack [src: Wikipedia, Atmospheric river].
- West/northwest flow reduces orographic lift and spill-over; cutoff lows are unpredictable; split troughs send energy south [MS].
- Gaps in the crest matter: Mammoth sits in a low gap in the crest, which helps explain its snowfall vs. nearby east-side peaks [src: Wikipedia, Mammoth Mountain].
- Tahoe west-slope resorts (Palisades, Kirkwood, Sugar Bowl) get direct upslope; east side and Nevada side are drier, rain-shadowed [GK].

## Snow-killers
- Warm ARs ("Pineapple Express") with high snow levels: rain on the lower mountain and rapid melt [src: Wikipedia, Pineapple Express; GK].
- Wind: lift holds usually at sustained 45–55 mph or gusts >65 mph (Mammoth) [MS]; wind packs and strips upper terrain [MS].
- Long ridges: dry spells of 2–4 weeks, often in January [MS].
- Spring sun: high sun angle makes snow wet and sticky [MS].

## Elevation and snow levels
- Base elevations roughly 6,000–9,000 ft, summits to ~11,000 ft (Mammoth Main Lodge 8,900 ft) [MS; GK].
- Snow levels swing widely: "base-type" storms bring snow to 4,000–6,500 ft; warm storms can push snow levels to 7,000–9,000 ft [MS examples]. Tahoe's lower bases are more rain-prone than Mammoth [GK].

## Seasonality
- February & March are usually the biggest months at Mammoth [MS]. Season roughly early Nov–Memorial Day there, longer in big years [MS].
- Averages: Mammoth about 400 in [src: Wikipedia] (400–500 in, per MS); Palisades Tahoe about 400 in [src: Wikipedia].

## Powder-day profile
- Big, infrequent dumps of dense snow; best days often the clearing day after a storm, when lifts reopen [MS].
- Fresh snow on top of a dense base skis well; storm days often bring wind holds and limited terrain [MS].
- Low-end powder from 2–5 in on limited terrain early season [MS].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- El Niño leans wetter for the southern US/southern California; northern Sierra impacts are weaker and mixed [src: NOAA climate.gov ENSO page; GK].
- Negative PNA favors western troughing, colder and wetter Sierra storms; MJO can steer the jet toward CA within 7–14 days [MS].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Maritime: deep, relatively uniform snowpack; avalanche danger peaks during and right after storms and settles quickly; wet-snow problems in spring [src: Avalanche.org maritime page; GK].
- Centers: Sierra Avalanche Center https://www.sierraavalanchecenter.org/ · Eastern Sierra Avalanche Center https://www.esavalanche.org/

## Example resorts
- **Palisades Tahoe**: west-slope Tahoe; summit 9,010 ft, ~400 in average [src: Wikipedia].
- **Mammoth Mountain**: east-side crest gap, high base; ~400 in [src: Wikipedia].
- **Kirkwood**: high base and deep snow south of Tahoe [GK].
