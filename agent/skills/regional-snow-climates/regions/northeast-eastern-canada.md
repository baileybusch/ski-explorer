# Northeast US / Eastern Canada

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate.

## Climate type
- **Humid continental** with frequent freeze–thaw, plus **lake effect** downwind of the Great Lakes [src: Wikipedia, Lake-effect snow; GK].

## Snow-to-liquid and density
- Highly variable: lake-effect and post-frontal upslope snow can be very light (15:1–25:1 in cold air); nor'easters and mixed storms dense (~8:1–12:1) or mixed with sleet/freezing rain [GK, approx.].

## Storm setups and flow
- **Nor'easters**: coastal lows bring heavy rain or snow, blizzard conditions and strong winds; snow is favored from Boston northward/inland, with track deciding rain vs. snow [src: Wikipedia, Nor'easter].
- **Lake effect**: cold air over warmer lake water creates narrow intense bands, usually within ~40 km of the shore, sometimes up to ~150 km; needs instability, fetch and limited wind shear [src: Wikipedia; NWS lake-effect page].
- **Northwest upslope** behind fronts: Green Mountains (Jay Peak, Stowe, Smugglers' Notch) catch Lake Ontario/Champlain moisture and orographic lift [src: Wikipedia, Jay Peak (orographic lift and proximity to water); GK].
- Clippers (fast, small Canadian lows) add modest, light snow [GK].

## Snow-killers
- **Rain and mixed precip** from inland-tracking lows and warm sectors; "January thaws" [GK].
- **Ice**: freezing rain coats slopes, then refreezes boilerplate [src: Wikipedia, Ice storm for mechanism; ski impacts GK].
- Wind holds on exposed summits; extreme cold (Mount Washington's notorious weather) [GK].

## Elevation and freezing levels
- Low mountains: bases ~1,000–2,000 ft, summits mostly 3,000–4,400 ft [GK, approx.]. Rain–snow line often within the vertical of a single resort [GK].

## Seasonality
- Peak natural snow roughly Jan–Mar; snowmaking critical early season [GK].
- Jay Peak claims ~359 in annually (ZRankings estimates 322 in), the most in eastern North America [src: Wikipedia].

## Powder-day profile
- Best days: cold NW upslope or lake-effect bands after a storm, or a cold nor'easter that stays all snow. Woods skiing holds powder; trails get scraped fast [GK].
- Check for rain/ice before the storm — new snow on ice skis poorly [GK].

## ENSO and teleconnections
- ENSO signal is weak/mixed in the Northeast [src: NOAA climate.gov map; GK]. **NAO/AO** matter more: negative NAO/AO favors cold and coastal storm tracks [src: NOAA CPC NAO/AO pages; GK].

## Avalanche and snowpack
- Mostly on-piste concerns (ice, thin cover). Backcountry avalanche terrain is limited, mainly Mount Washington's ravines and Chic-Chocs in Québec [GK].
