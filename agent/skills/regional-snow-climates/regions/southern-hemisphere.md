# Southern Hemisphere — New Zealand, Chile/Argentina (season roughly June–October)

Tags: **[src]** = linked source; **[GK]** = general knowledge, approximate. Optional region — thinner sourcing.

## New Zealand (Southern Alps)
- **Climate**: maritime; strong westerlies cross the Southern Alps [GK].
- **Setups**: **Southerly** cold outbreaks bring the best snow (cold, lower snow levels) to Canterbury and Queenstown-area fields [GK]. Northwesterly flow pours rain onto the West Coast and sends hot, dry foehn winds (the "nor'wester") down the eastern side, a snow-killer for Canterbury/Otago [GK; see Wikipedia, Foehn wind].
- **Snow quality**: often dense, wind-affected; occasional light snow in strong southerlies [GK].
- **Elevation**: fields ~1,300–2,000 m; small, above treeline [GK].
- **Sources**: MetService mountains and parks: https://www.metservice.com/mountains-and-parks · NZ Avalanche Advisory: https://www.avalanche.net.nz/ · NIWA: https://niwa.co.nz/ · OpenSnow NZ: https://opensnow.com/dailysnow/newzealand
- **ENSO**: during El Niño, NZ winter winds tend to blow more from the south (colder temperatures nationwide), with southwesterlies more common in spring/autumn; La Niña brings more northeasterlies [src: NIWA — https://niwa.co.nz/climate-and-weather/el-nino-and-la-nina]. Snow impact per field is [GK]. The Southern Annular Mode also shapes the westerlies [src: Wikipedia, SAM].
- **Examples**: Mt Hutt, Treble Cone, The Remarkables [GK].

## Chile / Argentina (Andes)
- **Climate**: central Chile's resorts see Mediterranean-style winters, with snow season June–September [src: Wikipedia, Climate of Chile]. Abundant Andean snow with moderate temperatures makes good skiing [src].
- **Setups**: Pacific fronts and cutoff lows from the west/northwest; central Andes depend on a few big storms [GK]. Patagonian Andes (Bariloche/Cerro Catedral) are wetter, more maritime [GK].
- **Snow-killers**: **Zonda** — foehn wind on the eastern (Argentine) slope [src: Wikipedia, Zonda]; warm storms with high snow levels; long dry spells; strong wind at high elevation [GK].
- **Elevation**: central Andes resorts are high (bases ~2,200–3,000 m; summits ~3,300–3,600 m+) [GK, approx.]; altitude sickness and sun/wind matter.
- **Snow quality**: high altitude and dry air can give light snow, but wind affects it quickly [GK].
- **Sources**: Dirección Meteorológica de Chile: https://www.meteochile.gob.cl/ · Servicio Meteorológico Nacional (Argentina): https://www.smn.gob.ar/ (blocked our checks) · OpenSnow South America: https://opensnow.com/dailysnow/southamerica
- **ENSO**: El Niño is commonly associated with wetter, snowier winters in central Chile; La Niña drier [GK — widely reported; verify per season].
- **Avalanche**: limited public bulletins; check resort/local services [GK].
- **Examples**: Portillo, Valle Nevado (Chile); Las Leñas, Cerro Catedral (Argentina) [GK].

## Notes for the agent
- Seasons are flipped: "peak" is roughly July–August [GK].
- Teleconnections differ: use SAM and ENSO, not NAO/PNA.
